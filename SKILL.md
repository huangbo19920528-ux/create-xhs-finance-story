# Create XHS Finance Story

## Goal

Turn a real financial or cross-border banking experience into a useful post a friend could follow. Preserve the user's voice and dated sequence of events. Verify changing rules, protect private data, and make the result readable on a phone. Do not let warnings, labels, or generic disclaimers overwhelm the story.

## Workflow

### 1. Start from the lived experience

Collect the smallest set of facts that explains the story:

- What happened, when, and where?
- What was the user's actual goal?
- Which attempts failed or succeeded?
- What changed between attempts?
- Which details came from the user, an official source, or another person's experience?
- Which personal details must be rounded, removed, or redacted?

Build a private fact table with four evidence types: `official rule`, `user observation`, `experience reference`, and `inference`. Use these labels for reasoning, but do not mechanically print them in every paragraph.

Write the opening as a compact first-person scene: date or recentness, action, difficulty, result, and why the note may help. Prefer concrete language such as “我第一次……，后来……” over a generic tutorial voice.

### 2. Verify unstable facts

Browse current official sources for fees, thresholds, eligibility, documents, product charges, deposit terms, app support, tax, board lots, and other details that may change. Record the page title, direct URL, applicable scope, and verification date.

Use community posts only to discover questions or describe what the user read. Do not turn a repeated community suggestion into an official rule. Keep the date of the lived experience separate from the date of source verification.

Read [research-and-costing.md](references/research-and-costing.md) when the post includes official rules, troubleshooting claims, product comparisons, or calculations.

### 3. Separate sequence from causation

Describe exactly what changed and what happened next. A successful attempt after changing an app version, network, declared amount, email, address, or bundled product does not prove that the change caused approval.

Keep the uncertainty natural and brief. Usually one sentence is enough:

> 这是我的操作顺序，不能证明因果；随后成功也可能有其他审核因素。

Do not repeat this sentence after every page. Never invent facts, recommend false declarations, or turn slang and euphemisms into a method for bypassing platform review.

### 4. Choose one clear story arc

For a first-person troubleshooting post, default to:

1. Result and the few changes the user made
2. The most relevant current official condition
3. First changed variable and observed result
4. Second changed variable and observed result
5. Third changed variable and observed result
6. App, support, or operating detail
7. The final successful sequence and remaining uncertainty
8. Official sources, verification date, and a short content note

For an account-opening walkthrough, use prerequisites, application steps, identity/address verification, card or delivery choices, activation, and post-opening setup. For an investment decision, use the user's constraint, available routes, actual choice, alternatives, comparable costs, break-even points, and treatment of uninvested cash.

Keep one main point per page. Adapt the outline to the story instead of forcing every topic into the same template.

### 5. Write like a person, not a compliance template

Create one plain title plus two backups, a first-person body divided by `图N｜标题`, 4–7 relevant hashtags, and an AI-assistance note when applicable.

Apply these style rules:

- Keep the user's natural wording when it is clear and accurate.
- Lead with what the user did and saw; put explanations after the event.
- Use short paragraphs and familiar words.
- Prefer “我这次……” and “我后来……” to “用户应当……” or “必须……”.
- State a direct conclusion only when it is supported; otherwise frame it as the user's own sequence or suspicion.
- Use one necessary boundary sentence near the relevant claim or at the end.
- Avoid repeated disclaimers, repeated risk language, bureaucratic labels, inflated transitions, and formulaic “不是……而是……” constructions.
- Do not list unrelated prohibited activities merely to announce that the post does not offer them.

Verify the current publishing limits in the app. If unavailable, keep the title comfortably short and target at most about 900 Chinese characters for a 1,000-character body so hashtags and one short note still fit. Count the exact pasted version, including line breaks, hashtags, and disclosures.

Read [cards-copy-and-privacy.md](references/cards-copy-and-privacy.md) for copy patterns, page templates, visual QA, privacy checks, and publishing settings.

### 6. Handle review feedback by meaning

When a platform flags a post, identify the semantic category, the exact text spans, and the exact images it highlighted. Revise only what is necessary while preserving the truthful story.

- Remove unnecessary actionable detail from sensitive financial topics.
- Replace unsupported universal advice with dated first-person experience.
- Move changing eligibility or fee details to an official-source card when that improves clarity.
- Prefer a direct official link over copying a long block of terms.
- Appeal when the content is accurate and the classification appears mistaken.

Do not treat synonym substitution or code words as a reliable review strategy. Do not make the prose harder for ordinary readers merely to avoid a keyword.

### 7. Protect privacy

Use rounded public scenarios rather than real balances or exact transaction amounts. Remove names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, account and order identifiers, QR codes, barcodes, notification text, device identifiers, timestamps, session tokens, metadata, and local paths.

Do not publish the user's original screenshots inside a public skill repository. If screenshots are used in a post, inspect the exported images again after redaction.

### 8. Produce mobile-readable cards

Use an approved visual system consistently. Default to a calm blue-led editorial or restrained hand-drawn style, with large legible Chinese body text, sans-serif English and numbers, few decorations, and no large dead zones.

When exact text, figures, codes, or URLs matter, render them with editable layout tools rather than inside an AI-generated illustration. Prefer an editable source plus ordered portrait PNG exports.

Lock page 1 before making the full set when the style is unsettled. Then inspect every page at phone scale and correct overflow, off-center numbers, weak contrast, inconsistent title sizes, tiny body copy, empty lower areas, and frames that do not contain their text.

### 9. Run the final check

Confirm:

- The dated personal timeline is still the center of the post.
- Current rules and figures have direct official sources and a verification date.
- Observed sequence is not presented as proven causation.
- The copy sounds like the user, not an AI-generated disclaimer.
- The body and title fit the current limits with buffer.
- No private data appears in text, images, filenames, metadata, or editable sources.
- Every card is readable without zooming and matches the `图N` body section.
- Product comparisons use like-for-like assumptions and do not promise returns.
- The final page contains readable source titles and direct URLs.
- Any closing note is brief, relevant, and true.

## Deliverables

Return only what the task needs, normally:

1. one recommended title and two backups;
2. a publish-ready body with hashtags;
3. an 8-page card script;
4. final portrait images and an editable source when requested;
5. a privacy checklist;
6. an official-source list with verification date;
7. a cost or break-even table only when the story actually needs one.

The final result should feel like a careful friend sharing a recent, verifiable experience—not a sales pitch, a legal notice, or a guaranteed recipe.
